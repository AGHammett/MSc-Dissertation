Score-based Generative Modelling with Diffusion Processes
Supervisor: Dr. Oliver Feng, o.feng@lse.ac.uk
In machine learning, the aim of generative modelling is to obtain artificial samples that are similar (in
distribution) to those in a training dataset, which is often a large database of high-dimensional objects
such as images or videos. There has recently been a surge of interest in procedures that incrementally
add noise to the training examples and continually learn the *score function* of the noisy data,
namely the derivative of its log-likelihood. The estimated scores are then used to transform pure noise
back into a new sample, via Langevin dynamics or more generally a diffusion process defined by a
stochastic differential equation. Neural network implementations of scorebased algorithms have
achieved remarkable empirical success, often outperforming other state-of the art methods like
generative adversarial networks.
The goal of the project is to explore this rapidly growing field, which has gained significant traction not
only in the artificial intelligence community but also among statisticians seeking theoretical
guarantees. The student is expected to give a detailed mathematical exposition of score-based
generative modelling, including the fundamentals of *score matching* as a key ingredient. One
possible follow-up direction is to apply and/or adapt the methodology to a generative modelling task
of the student’s choice, using simulated or real data. It may be of interest to investigate extensions of
score matching to data on different domains. An alternative option is to survey and perhaps to extend
some recent mathematical results on diffusion processes and their convergence properties, or the
accuracy of the samples generated in a nonparametric context
