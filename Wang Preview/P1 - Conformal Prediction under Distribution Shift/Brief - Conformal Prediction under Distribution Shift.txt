Conformal Prediction under Distribution Shift
Supervisor: Professor Tengyao Wang, t.wang59@lse.ac.uk
Conformal prediction provides distribution-free prediction sets with finite-sample marginal coverage
guarantees under exchangeability (Vovk et al., 2005). However, this assumption is often violated in
practice: the population encountered after deployment may differ from that used to train and calibrate
the model. Standard conformal intervals can consequently lose their coverage guarantees.
Recent methods address this problem under different assumptions about the shift. Under covariate
shift, importance-weighted conformal prediction reweights calibration observations to represent the
target population (Tibshirani et al., 2019). Other approaches provide guarantees under nonexchangeable data, use doubly robust calibration, or protect against distributions in a specified
neighbourhood of the training distribution (Barber et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2024; Cauchois et al., 2024).
Important practical questions concern estimating importance weights, limited overlap between
populations, and the trade-off between coverage and interval width.
In the project, the student will review conformal prediction under distribution shift, implement and
compare standard, importance-weighted and robust methods through simulations and real datasets.
Simulations may vary the severity of covariate shift, sample size, heteroscedasticity and source–target
overlap. Capable students may explore weight truncation, doubly robust calibration, or departures
from the covariate-shift assumption.
Prerequisites:
 A solid background in mathematical statistics, including regression, hypothesis testing and
asymptotic ideas.
 Good computational skills in R or Python, including implementing algorithms, running
simulations and analysing real datasets.
 Optional but desirable: prior exposure to statistical learning, importance sampling or
semiparametric methods.
