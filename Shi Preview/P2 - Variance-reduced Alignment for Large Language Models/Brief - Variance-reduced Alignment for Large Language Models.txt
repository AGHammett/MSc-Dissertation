Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) has become a gold standard for aligning large
language models (LLMs) with human preferences. However, most existing RLHF algorithms rely on the
Bradley–Terry (BT) model, which makes unrealistic assumptions about human preferences that often
fail to capture their complexity and variability in real-world settings.
Ye et al. (2025) recently proposed variance-reduced preference optimization (VRPO; see references),
an algorithm designed to improve the sample efficiency and robustness of existing RLHF methods
under model misspecification. They demonstrated VRPO's strong empirical performance on the IMDb,
Helpful–Harmless (HH), and TL;DR datasets.
This project aims to further advance VRPO by improving its computational and statistical efficiency and
by extending its application to the UltraFeedback dataset, which is more complex than the three
aforementioned datasets in both data scale and response complexity. The project will be
codingintensive. Its successful completion is expected to lead to a research paper suitable for
submission to a (top-tier) machine learning conference or statistical journal
