What Does a Human-Preference Leaderboard Actually Rank?
Supervisor: Professor Milan Vojnović, m.vojnovic@lse.ac.uk
Abstract:
Leaderboards built from human votes between pairs of model responses, such as Chatbot Arena (now
LMArena), are widely used to compare models and to inform decisions about their development and
adoption. Rankings are computed by pooling votes from a self-selected crowd of users into a single
score for each model. In effect, a model’s score reflects how often it wins against the other models it is
shown against, averaged over voters. If voters disagree, however, the resulting ranking need not match
what any particular group of users would conclude, and it may depend on which pairs of models
happen to be shown. This project asks what such a ranking actually measures and which ranking claims
can be trusted for other user populations. The student will characterise what the pooled ranking
estimates when voters are heterogeneous, derive bounds on how a model’s rank could change under
different or uncertain mixes of users, and develop confidence statements of the form ‘model A ranks
above model B for every plausible user mix’. The methods will be checked by simulation, applied to
publicly released leaderboard votes using language and prompt type as observable user segments, and
tested on a dataset in which the voter groups are known. The work combines theory, simulation and
data analysis, and suits a student with a strong grounding in likelihood theory and an interest in the
statistical evaluation of AI systems
